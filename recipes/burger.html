How to make a Burger

We are going to master the classic backyard smash burger, a legendary technique highly praised by the online food community on platforms like Reddit’s r/burgers for creating maximum flavor with minimal effort. Instead of a thick, standard patty, you will take loosely formed balls of high-fat ground beef and press them firmly into a smoking hot cast-iron skillet using a heavy spatula. This intense pressure creates a massive amount of surface contact with the heat, triggering a rapid Maillard reaction that locks in juices and yields those signature, highly-coveted ultra-crispy, lacy edges. In just a few fast-paced minutes, you will toast your buns, melt a slice of gooey American cheese over the seared meat, and stack them up with your favorite quick toppings for a restaurant-quality meal right at home.

Ingredients required

  • 80/20 Ground Beef
  • Potato Buns
  • American Cheese
  • Salt and Black Pepper
  • Burger Sauce
  • Dill Pickle slices

Steps

  1. Prep the Skillet & Beef
    • Preheat your pan: Set a cast-iron skillet or heavy frying pan over medium-high heat until it is smoking hot.
    • Form the patties: Divide 8 ounces of ground beef into two equal balls and gently press them into patties slightly wider than your buns.
  2. Season & Sear
    • Season generously: Coat both sides of the beef patties with a heavy pinch of kosher salt and black pepper right before cooking.
    • Sear the beef: Place the patties in the hot skillet and cook undisturbed for 3 to 4 minutes until a deep, brown crust forms.
  3. Layer the Toppings
    • Melt the cheese: Flip the burgers, immediately place a slice of cheese on each patty, and cook for 2 more minutes until melted.
    • Toast the buns: Spread a thin layer of butter or mayonnaise on the burger buns and toast them face-down in the pan until golden.
    • Assemble the burger: Place the cheesy patty on the bottom bun, then top with a dollop of burger sauce, lettuce, and tomato slices.
  4. Rest & Serve
    • Rest the meat: Transfer the assembled burgers to a plate and let them sit for 2 minutes so the juices redistribute throughout the meat.
    • Serve hot: Enjoy your freshly made burger immediately while the cheese is gooey and the bun is perfectly warm.
